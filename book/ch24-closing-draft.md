I told you this book started at a kitchen table in Orinda, with a tax return open on a laptop and a number that would not cooperate. Two hundred sixty thousand dollars a year, and the math still said we could not stop running. I said it out loud that night because I expected it to sound absurd. It sounded like arithmetic.

[[One or two true sentences here: the decision you made in the weeks after that night, and the shape of the leap. Keep it plain. A date, a flight, a lease.]]

This morning the arithmetic is different. [[One concrete present-day detail: what you can see or hear from where you sit now, first thing in the day.]] The numbers are smaller than they were in Orinda, and they cover more. That is the part I could not see from inside the old system. You do not simply spend less when you change the ground under your feet. You get back the things the spending was supposed to buy, and you get them years earlier than the plan ever promised.

For a long time I believed the location was fixed and everything else was the variable. I negotiated raises. I chased deductions. I moved money between accounts and called it strategy. All of it happened inside a set of walls I never thought to question, and the walls were charging rent on my whole life. The day I finally treated the map as a choice instead of a given, every other number on the page started to move.

I want to be honest about what the move did and did not do. It did not make me a different person. It did not solve the ordinary problems that follow you across any border. What it did was hand back the hours. The morning that used to belong to a commute. The Tuesday afternoon that used to belong to someone else's calendar. The slow dinner that used to cost three hundred dollars and now costs a fraction of that, with better light. Those hours are the actual wealth. The money was only ever the way we kept score.

That is the promise underneath the whole book, and it is the one that took me longest to trust. Where you live sets the price of your money, the size of your time, and the room you have to become the person you meant to be. Change the where, and you change all three at once.
