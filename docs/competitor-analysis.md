# Vamos San Miguel: Competitor and Content-Gap Analysis

Prepared for the "Vamos San Miguel" project (bilingual events + curated local picks + relocation guide).
Date of research: August 2026.

## How to read this document

Every factual claim about a competitor below is tied to a page that was actually opened during research (via WebFetch or the browser pane). Where a source could not be opened, the claim is labeled UNVERIFIED and explained. No traffic numbers, revenue figures, or audience sizes are asserted as fact unless a source stated them, and those are attributed. This project has not fabricated metrics.

Sites that blocked automated access and could not be opened for direct confirmation: Culture Trip, the standalone Que Pasa website (quepasasanmiguel.com would not resolve or navigate), and the private Facebook groups (login-walled). These are flagged in place.

## 1. The competitive landscape

### A. Local news and events (the incumbents)

**Atención San Miguel + Que Pasa (atencionsanmiguel.org / print weekly)**
- What it covers: The town's long-running weekly newspaper plus its "Que Pasa" what-to-do supplement, which functions as the community's default weekly cultural calendar (art, community, culture, opinion, NGO activity).
- Language: Bilingual (English and Spanish).
- History and model: Founded in August 1975; published by La Biblioteca Pública, a local NGO. Advertising in the paper covers newspaper costs and also funds most of the library's community programs; the paper prints roughly 3,000 copies in high season and 1,500 in low season and sells for about 20 pesos. It is described as the only local newspaper produced in San Miguel.
- Apparent strength: Deep local roots, trust, and the widest habitual reach for weekly cultural listings. Residents describe a Friday ritual of marking up Que Pasa to plan their week.
- Gaps and weaknesses: Print-first and weekly, so discovery is slow and not mobile-native. Editorially it is largely a community bulletin board: the editor states roughly 80 percent of editorial content is generated by volunteer contributors who are "free to promote virtually any self-interest," and the paper has been critiqued as "mundane and often sycophantic." So it is bilingual and trusted, but it is not curated, not opinionated in a quality sense, and not visually rich.
- Source (opened): Roslyn Bernstein, "Paying Attention: Atención Newspaper," Medium, March 2018. https://rbernst662.medium.com/paying-attention-92b757837fba
- Note: The standalone Que Pasa website (quepasasanmiguel.com) could not be opened during research (DNS/navigation failed repeatedly), so claims here about Que Pasa rest on the Medium interview with the paper's editor rather than the site itself. Flagged as a partial verification.

**San Miguel Times (sanmigueltimes.com)**
- What it covers: English-language news site. Sections are San Miguel, Travel & Leisure, Food & Drink, Business, National, Guanajuato, Querétaro, Real Estate, Health, and World.
- Language: English only.
- Content pattern: The category counts on the site skew heavily toward general news and reposted national/world stories (Headlines 3,286, National 1,376, World 849) versus local lifestyle (Food & Drink 195, Travel & Leisure 571). It reads as a news aggregator with a San Miguel banner rather than a curated local guide, and it carries no events calendar.
- Apparent strength: Steady English-language news volume and SEO presence.
- Gaps and weaknesses: English only, wire-style and reposted content, no curation of things to do, no bilingual reach, no original local media brand.
- Source (opened, via browser pane): https://sanmigueltimes.com/ (homepage and category listing read directly).

**San Miguel Live (sanmiguellive.com)**
- What it covers: A searchable, filterable events calendar (live music, classical and opera, theater and dance, film, workshops, art openings, festivals, dining), with venue and performer profiles.
- Language: Trilingual (English, Spanish, French toggles in the header).
- Monetization: Banner advertising (sponsors visible include a real-estate brokerage and local businesses) plus event-submission and login functionality, suggesting a free-plus-paid listing model.
- Apparent strength: The most events-focused, filterable calendar in the market; multilingual.
- Gaps and weaknesses: Dated design and weak visual hierarchy; feels like an older listings database rather than a modern, mobile-first product; curation is thin (it is a calendar, not a point of view).
- Source (opened): https://www.sanmiguellive.com/

### B. Directories and "discover" platforms

**Discover San Miguel de Allende (discoversma.com)**
- What it covers: A broad local directory plus events. Business directory (arts, restaurants, health and beauty, real estate, professional services, retail, travel), a medical directory (physicians by specialty, hospitals, dentists, pharmacies), an events calendar by category, plus utilities (maps, bus routes, emergency contacts, webcam).
- Language: Bilingual (English and Spanish interface).
- Monetization: Explicitly an advertising business. It self-describes as "San Miguel Marketing and Advertising Services" and sells featured event listings, featured business listings, and tiered advertising packages.
- Apparent strength: Breadth and utility; bilingual; the medical and services directories are genuinely useful.
- Gaps and weaknesses: Pay-to-play by design, so listings signal who paid, not who is good. Utilitarian rather than curated or beautiful; no distinct editorial voice; no honest "best of" position.
- Source (opened): https://discoversma.com/

### C. Expat community and relocation

**San Miguel Newcomers (sanmiguelnewcomers.com)**
- What it covers: A free community for English-speaking expats, retirees, and newcomers. Regular in-person events (dinner clubs, lunch-and-learns, book groups, day trips), a vetted resource directory (real estate, healthcare, legal, home services), member directory, Q&A, and relocation guides (cost of living, healthcare, neighborhoods, safety, residency).
- Language: English only.
- Monetization: States membership is free. Notably, the resource directory states "No one can pay to be listed," positioning it as an honest, non-advertising directory. The page states the community has operated since 2010 and is part of a broader "Mexico Newcomers" network (attributed on-page to Panoramic Media LLC). It cites 30,000-plus Facebook followers.
- Apparent strength: Trust and honesty in its directory; warm community-first brand; the single closest competitor to Vamos San Miguel's "never paid for" ethos.
- Gaps and weaknesses: English only (no Spanish, no bilingual reach), oriented to services and social connection rather than curated culture, dining, and things to do; not a visual or events-discovery product; audience is retirees and newcomers, not visitors or younger travelers.
- Source (opened): https://sanmiguelnewcomers.com/events

**Mexico Relocation Guide (mexicorelocationguide.com)**
- What it covers: Relocation content for all of Mexico (visas, cost of living, healthcare, a vetted contacts directory of immigration facilitators, realtors, lawyers, movers), sold primarily through a paid online course ("Complete Mexico Relocation Guide") plus private relocation tours and a YouTube presence. San Miguel is one destination among many, not the focus.
- Language: English (no Spanish on the page reviewed).
- Monetization: Paid course, private tours, email lead generation, likely affiliate and referral income.
- Apparent strength: Strong national brand for the "how to move to Mexico" search intent; the paid-course funnel is well built.
- Gaps and weaknesses: Not San Miguel specific, English only, and structured as a sales funnel rather than an editorially independent local guide. Depth on San Miguel neighborhoods, culture, and daily life is shallow relative to a dedicated local product.
- Source (opened): https://mexicorelocationguide.com/living-in-san-miguel-de-allende-mexico/

**Falling in Love with San Miguel (fallinginlovewithsanmiguel.com)**
- What it covers: A combined travel-and-relocation guide blog (visas, housing, neighborhood profiles for Centro, San Antonio, Guadalupe, cost of living, attractions, cuisine, events).
- Voice: Has a named author (Ellie Thomson) and an insider, experiential tone, which is rare in this set.
- Language: English only.
- Monetization: Multiple affiliate and referral channels (book-a-hotel and book-a-flight affiliate links, tour and excursion partnerships, immigration and real-estate referrals).
- Apparent strength: Named voice plus a relocation-and-travel blend, which is the same lane Vamos San Miguel wants; good SEO structure.
- Gaps and weaknesses: English only; monetized through affiliate and referral links, which undercuts an "honest, never paid" claim; standard blog stock-style presentation rather than original photography; no events product and no interactive tooling.
- Source (opened): https://fallinginlovewithsanmiguel.com/san-miguel-de-allende-essential-relocation-guide/

- General strengths and weaknesses of this category: High trust and immediacy, but content is ephemeral, unsearchable over time, English-dominant, unstructured, and impossible to plan a trip or a move from. This is where demand leaks because no polished product captures it.

## 2. Where the gaps are

Reading across the field, several gaps are real and defensible. No single competitor closes more than one or two of these.

1. Nobody is fully bilingual AND high quality. The bilingual options (Atención/Que Pasa, San Miguel Live, Discover) are either print-first, a dated calendar, or a pay-to-play directory. The high-effort editorial voices (Falling in Love, Newcomers, Mexico Relocation Guide) are English only. True English-and-Spanish parity with quality on both sides is unclaimed.

2. Nobody combines honesty with curation and beauty. Discover and San Miguel Live are advertising-driven, so their prominence signals who paid. Atención's editorial is largely volunteer self-promotion by its own editor's account. Falling in Love runs on affiliate and referral links. The one honest-directory exception, San Miguel Newcomers ("no one can pay to be listed"), is English only, services-focused, and not a visual or culture-and-dining product. An editorially independent, "never paid for," visually rich curation of the best places does not exist.

3. Nobody owns original photography and a distinctive visual brand. Directories are utilitarian, blogs use standard stock-style imagery, and international listicles reuse generic photos. There is no local brand whose original photography and drone work make it instantly recognizable and screenshot-worthy.

4. Events discovery is stuck in the print and Web-1.0 era. The habitual calendar (Que Pasa) is a weekly print supplement; the digital calendar (San Miguel Live) is dated; the directory calendar (Discover) is cluttered. There is no modern, mobile-first, bilingual, genuinely pleasant way to find what is on this week.

5. There is no interactive or AI-assisted planning anywhere. Every competitor is a static calendar, listicle, directory, or forum. Nobody turns "I have three days and I like art and wine" into a plan. This is greenfield.

6. Relocation content is fragmented and conflicted. It is split between an all-Mexico paid-course funnel (Mexico Relocation Guide) and affiliate/referral blogs and agency-adjacent guides (Falling in Love, and real-estate-run relocation pages surfaced in search). None is a San Miguel-specific, editorially independent, book-depth relocation resource that is not primarily a lead funnel for real estate or immigration services.

7. No brand connects all three jobs. Visitors, weekly locals, and prospective movers are served by different, disconnected sites. Nobody offers one trusted bilingual brand that carries a person from "should I visit" to "what is on this week" to "how do I move here."

## 3. Where Vamos San Miguel can win

Vamos San Miguel's stated advantages map almost one-to-one onto the gaps above, which is the strongest possible sign of product-market fit in a crowded field.

- Fully bilingual: directly answers Gap 1. It can be the quality option in Spanish and English at once, which none of the strong editorial players do.
- Named insider voice: answers part of Gap 2 and Gap 7. Only Falling in Love has a named voice, and it is English only and affiliate-monetized. A trusted, bilingual, named local is a durable brand asset that directories and wire-news sites structurally cannot copy.
- Original photos and drone: answers Gap 3 outright. This is the cheapest way to look categorically better than every incumbent and to be shareable.
- Honest "never paid for" picks: answers Gap 2 and is the trust wedge. Only San Miguel Newcomers holds a comparable honesty claim, and it is English only and not a culture-and-dining product. Vamos can own "independent and never paid" across dining, culture, and day trips, in two languages.
- AI itinerary builder: answers Gap 5, where there is literally no competition. It converts the curated picks and events feed into a plan, which is a feature no incumbent has.
- Book-derived relocation pillar: answers Gap 6. A San Miguel-specific, book-depth, editorially independent relocation resource beats both the all-Mexico paid course and the affiliate blogs on depth, specificity, and trust, and it feeds naturally from the same bilingual brand.

The combination is the moat. Any one competitor could bolt on one of these. None is positioned to be bilingual, honest, beautiful, interactive, and relocation-deep at the same time, because their business models (advertising directories, volunteer bulletin, wire news, affiliate blogs, paid courses) pull against it.

## 4. Positioning and content recommendations

### Positioning statement (draft)

"The honest, bilingual guide to San Miguel: the events, the places worth your time, and the real story of living here. Every recommendation is independent, never paid for, and shown through our own photography. In English and Spanish."

This claims the three unoccupied high grounds at once: bilingual parity, editorial independence ("never paid for"), and visual quality, wrapped around a named local voice.

### What to build first (in priority order)

1. The honest curated-picks core (the trust and beauty wedge).
Build 15 to 25 cornerstone "best of" picks (restaurants, cafes, galleries, a few day trips) in full English and Spanish, each with original photography and drone, and each carrying a visible, consistent "independent, never paid for" mark. This is the single most differentiating, lowest-cost thing to ship, and it is defensible against every ad-driven and affiliate-driven incumbent. Lead the brand with it.

2. A modern bilingual events experience with a weekly digest.
Replace the print-era Que Pasa habit and the dated San Miguel Live experience with a mobile-first, filterable, bilingual calendar, plus a short weekly "what to actually do this week" digest (email and web) in both languages that reflects the named voice and a point of view, not just a raw list. The weekly digest is the retention engine and the habit-former.

3. The AI itinerary builder, seeded by 1 and 2.
Ship a simple planner that takes trip length and interests and returns a day-by-day plan drawn from the honest picks and the live events feed, in the user's language. This is the feature no competitor has and the clearest reason to choose Vamos over a calendar or a listicle. Keep the first version narrow (a few interest tags, one to four days) and make it obviously powered by the curated, independent picks so the AI inherits the trust.

4. The relocation pillar from the book (the depth and reach play).
Publish 6 to 10 cornerstone relocation chapters adapted from the book (visas and residency, neighborhoods, cost of living, healthcare, buying versus renting, daily life), bilingual, and pointedly editorially independent rather than a real-estate or immigration lead funnel. This captures the high-intent "move to San Miguel" search demand that Mexico Relocation Guide and agency blogs monetize, but with more local depth and more trust, and it feeds the same brand.

5. The named-voice trust page.
A strong "about the voice behind Vamos" page that makes the insider real and human. This is what converts a nice-looking site into a trusted one, and it is the thing directories, wire news, and international listicles cannot manufacture.

### Sequencing logic

Ship 1 and 2 first because they create the visible quality gap and the weekly habit. Add 3 to create a feature no one can match. Layer 4 to capture high-value relocation intent and to justify the book. Keep the "never paid for" and bilingual promises absolute across all of it, because that honesty and parity are the two things the entire incumbent field structurally cannot offer.
