# Housing in San Miguel de Allende: Renting and Buying

The house you pick is the multiplier on everything else you build here. Get it right and daily life runs quietly in the background. Get it wrong and you spend your energy managing the house instead of living in it. In San Miguel that starts with a real understanding of the neighborhoods, which vary more than newcomers expect, from the cobblestone lanes of the Centro Histórico to the wider, car-friendly streets of Los Frailes. The good news is that the mistakes are learnable in advance.

## How do rentals actually work in San Miguel de Allende?

The best long-term rentals do not appear on the sites you already know. They live with local realtors who hold quiet relationships with owners, in the San Miguel rental Facebook groups where "se renta" posts move within hours, and on the walls of the neighborhood you want, where owners still hang paper signs. If you search only the familiar international sites, you are seeing the leftovers. Contact at least two realtors, since they tend to represent different portfolios, and weight how connected they are above how much you personally click with them.

When you reach out, be specific about property type, monthly budget, furnishing level, kitchen reality, neighborhood, and space. Mexican "furnished" ranges from complete down to the silverware to a bed and a table, so define your terms. Then go see every serious contender in person, in daylight and after dark, on a weekday and a weekend, because photos lie with a straight face about space, light, and noise, and a Centro apartment charming at noon can sit above a loud bar at night. If a landlord will allow it, a one-week trial before signing surfaces what a viewing never will.

## Which neighborhood should I choose?

San Miguel is compact, but the neighborhoods have distinct characters and price levels. The Centro Histórico and its immediate edges are the most walkable and the most expensive. Guadiana and Ojo de Agua sit just south, leafy and central, popular with families and long-term residents. San Antonio, just over the hill from Centro, has grown into a diverse, food-forward neighborhood at more reasonable rents. Farther out you trade walkability for space and value.

## What is the Mexico-specific wrinkle in a rental?

Ask who pays for gardening and exterior maintenance, which is almost always the landlord, and whether an existing housekeeper comes with the house. In some rentals the landlord already has a trusted housekeeper on fixed days whom you pay directly, often around 60 to 90 pesos an hour. Those answers give you a truer picture of your real monthly cost and your weekly time savings, so ask three plain questions: who pays for gardening and exterior maintenance, is there an existing housekeeper and who pays them, and can you opt out of regular housekeeping if you would rather.

## What should I know before signing the lease?

Mexican rental agreements run on different assumptions than American ones. Get the lease in both English and Spanish, and understand that the Spanish version is the one that binds you, since a contract needs to be in Spanish to be enforceable here. Run both versions line by line to confirm they say the same thing, and fix any divergence before you sign.

Standard practice is one month's deposit plus first month's rent upfront. Negotiate the deposit-return timeline while you are at it, since landlords often propose 60 days and fifteen days after move-out is more reasonable. Put the payment method in the contract; transfers through a service like Wise create documentation, while cash leaves no paper trail. At higher price points, owners sometimes ask for a fiador, a local co-signer who owns debt-free property in the same state. Most new expats do not have one, and you can often negotiate around it with a larger deposit, several months paid upfront, or a letter of reference. Do not be shy about the rent itself, since Mexican landlords are often more flexible on price than their American counterparts, and the worst answer is no.

## Why does the exchange rate change my rent?

Most long-term leases quote and pay rent in pesos, which is usually what you want since your daily spending is in pesos too. The hidden variable is the exchange rate. A lease that feels affordable at today's rate turns more expensive in dollars if the peso strengthens, and the walls never move. You can stay in pesos and accept the swings, negotiate a defined band that adjusts annually, or in rare cases quote the rent in dollars directly. The point is to be intentional rather than stumble into a number that feels comfortable now and painful later.

## Can foreigners buy in San Miguel, and do I need a fideicomiso?

Here is the good news for buyers. The fideicomiso, the bank trust that foreigners must use to hold residential property, applies only inside Mexico's restricted zone: within 50 kilometers of the coast and 100 kilometers of an international border. San Miguel de Allende sits in the interior highlands of Guanajuato, well outside that zone, so foreigners can buy property here directly in their own name, in fee simple, with no bank trust required. That simplifies the purchase and removes the annual trust fee that coastal buyers pay. You still want a Mexican notario (a public notary, who in Mexico is a specialized attorney) to handle the closing, and a real-estate attorney is worth the cost.

Many people rent first, often fully furnished, to stay nimble while they learn the town and find the right spot before committing. Whether you rent or buy, the only real mistake is defaulting into a choice by accident instead of making it on purpose.

## What about the infrastructure reality?

Treat the infrastructure gap as part of the adjustment rather than a defect. Learn your specific water setup and confirm the rooftop tanks have been cleaned. Outlets and voltage match the United States, so your devices plug in and work, but power outages are more common than you are used to, so plan for battery backup from the beginning. Test actual internet speeds during peak hours rather than trusting the brochure number. In the Centro, factor in the cobblestones, which are beautiful and hard on ankles, luggage wheels, and low-clearance cars.

One more note: San Miguel's regional airports are the Querétaro airport (QRO), about an hour and a quarter away, and the León airport (BJX), roughly an hour and a half, and Querétaro is also the go-to for big-box shopping. Your relationship with your landlord runs well past the signing, and in Mexican culture personal relationships shape business outcomes, so pay on time, report issues clearly, and treat the property with obvious care.
