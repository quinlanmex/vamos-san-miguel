# Buying Property in Mexico and the Fideicomiso: Does It Apply in San Miguel?

Here is the good news for buyers in San Miguel de Allende. The fideicomiso, the bank trust that gets a lot of attention in articles about foreigners buying in Mexico, does not apply here. San Miguel sits in the interior highlands, outside the zone where the trust is required, so foreigners can buy directly in their own name. This page explains who can buy, what the fideicomiso actually is, and why it drops out of the picture inland.

## Can foreigners buy property in Mexico?

Yes. Foreigners can own residential property in Mexico. The one wrinkle is location. Mexico defines a restricted zone along its borders and coasts, and property inside that zone is held by foreigners through a bank trust rather than direct title. Everywhere outside the zone, foreigners buy in fee simple, holding direct title in their own name exactly as a Mexican citizen would.

## What is a fideicomiso?

A fideicomiso is a bank trust that lets a foreigner hold residential property inside Mexico's restricted zone. A Mexican bank holds the legal title as trustee, and you are the beneficiary with the full rights to use, rent, improve, sell, and pass on the property. It is a well-established, secure structure that has let foreigners own coastal and border homes for decades. It carries a setup cost and an annual trust fee paid to the bank.

The restricted zone is defined by distance from the border and the coast:

- Within 100 kilometers of an international border
- Within 50 kilometers of the coastline

Property inside either band requires the fideicomiso for foreign buyers. Property outside both does not.

## Does the fideicomiso apply in San Miguel de Allende?

No. San Miguel de Allende sits in the interior highlands of Guanajuato, well outside the restricted zone, so foreigners buy property here directly in their own name, in fee simple, with no bank trust required. That simplifies the purchase and removes the annual trust fee that coastal buyers pay. If you have read that buying in Mexico means setting up a bank trust, that guidance is written for the beach towns, not for an inland town like San Miguel.

## How does a purchase work inland?

Buying in San Miguel follows the standard Mexican process, minus the trust step. A few pieces are worth knowing before you start.

- **Use a notario.** A Mexican notario is a public notary, and in Mexico that is a specialized attorney who handles the closing, verifies title, calculates taxes, and records the deed. The notario is central to a valid transfer, not an optional add-on.
- **Consider a real-estate attorney.** Separate from the notario, a buyer's attorney reviewing the deal is worth the cost, especially on your first purchase in a new legal system.
- **Expect closing costs on top of price.** Closings carry taxes and fees beyond the purchase price. Ask your notario for the specific figures for your transaction rather than assuming a flat percentage.
- **Prices and costs move.** Confirm current numbers with local professionals and current listings before you rely on any figure.

## Should you rent before you buy?

Many people rent first, often fully furnished, to stay nimble while they learn the town and find the right spot before committing. Neighborhoods in San Miguel vary more than newcomers expect, from the cobblestone lanes of the Centro Histórico to the wider, car-friendly streets of Los Frailes, and a few months on the ground teaches you which one fits before you tie up capital. Whether you rent or buy, the only real mistake is defaulting into a choice by accident instead of making it on purpose.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need a fideicomiso to buy in San Miguel de Allende?** No. San Miguel is outside Mexico's restricted zone, so foreigners buy directly in fee simple with no bank trust.

**Where do you still need a fideicomiso?** Inside the restricted zone: within 50 kilometers of the coast or 100 kilometers of an international border. That covers the beach and border towns, not the interior highlands.

**Can I hold direct title as a foreigner inland?** Yes. Outside the restricted zone, foreigners hold direct fee-simple title in their own name, the same as a Mexican citizen.

**Do I need residency to buy?** Owning property does not require residency, and a debt-free property above a value threshold can even support a residency application. Confirm the specifics for your case with a professional.
