# The Cost of Living in San Miguel de Allende

San Miguel sits at about 6,200 feet in the highlands of Guanajuato, and the altitude does most of the work on the climate. The yearly average hovers around 68 degrees, afternoons stay mild, mornings and nights turn crisp, and there is no coastal humidity to fight. It is one of Mexico's most established expat towns, so you pay a premium for plug-and-play infrastructure and a ready-made community. Even with that premium, a couple can live comfortably here for roughly $1,800 to $2,500 a month all in, well under what the same life costs in most U.S. metros. Here is where the money actually goes.

## Housing

Rent is the single biggest swing in your budget, and in San Miguel it tracks almost entirely to neighborhood and how close you want to be to the Centro Histórico. A one-bedroom in the walkable heart of town runs roughly $700 to $1,200 a month unfurnished, and a two-bedroom in or near Centro commonly lands between $1,300 and $2,000. Step out to San Antonio, Guadalupe, or La Lejona and the same money buys more space. Furnished costs more, usually a premium of about $115 to $340 a month over the unfurnished version of the same place.

The best long-term rentals do not appear on the sites you already know. They live with local realtors who hold quiet relationships with owners, and in the San Miguel Facebook rental groups where "se renta" posts move fast. Contact at least two agents, weight how connected they are, and know that the classic move here still works: walk the neighborhood you want and look for signs on the wall. Standard practice is one month's deposit plus first month's rent upfront. Get the lease in both Spanish and English, and understand that the Spanish version is the one that binds you.

Rent is quoted and paid in pesos, which is what you want, but the exchange rate is a hidden variable in your real dollar cost. A lease that feels comfortable at 18 pesos to the dollar gets meaningfully more expensive if the peso strengthens, and the walls never move. Budget with a little cushion for that.

## Healthcare

Routine care is where Mexico is at its best, and you pay cash for the clarity of knowing the price before you walk in. A general-practitioner visit in San Miguel runs roughly $25 to $50, and a specialist $50 to $100, often less than a U.S. copay before anyone touches a deductible. San Miguel has solid local clinics and doctors, and for anything specialized or serious, the larger city of Querétaro is about an hour away with full hospitals.

For the bigger events, carry real coverage rather than going bare. Private health insurance for an expat commonly runs $1,500 to $3,000 a year per person depending on age and health history. A workable plan pays cash for routine care and carries insurance for catastrophic events and cross-border emergencies. Whether a U.S. plan with international benefits or a comprehensive Mexican or international plan wins depends on your situation, so get real quotes on your actual health.

## Domestic help

The math is what makes this reachable. Housekeeping runs roughly 60 to 90 pesos an hour, about $3 to $5, with a full day landing around $25 to $40. Gardeners commonly get in the range of 150 to 200 pesos for a day's work. For the price of a single U.S. deep clean, you can have help every week for a month, and the hours you buy back are a real part of why the move is worth making.

Budget for one obligation that is easy to miss. Mexican labor law requires an aguinaldo, a holiday bonus of at least 15 days' wages, paid by December 20, and it covers household staff. Treat it as a real cost, not a tip. Start with referrals from your landlord, who is your single most valuable resource on day one.

## Utilities

Mexican utilities work well, and they run on a logic you learn on purpose. Electricity comes from the CFE on a tiered structure that rewards low consumption and turns punishing at the top, so download the app on arrival and register your exact address. Most homes here need no air conditioning, which keeps the bill down; expect utilities together to run roughly $50 to $120 a month depending on the season and household. Gas is propane from a tank you order by WhatsApp when the gauge runs low, paid in cash. Treat tap water as not for drinking and budget for garrafones, the 20-liter jugs, at roughly 30 to 40 pesos each. Home internet is a bright spot: fiber packages run about $20 to $40 a month and are often faster and steadier than what many of us had in U.S. cities. A UPS for your modem plus a Starlink backup is worth it if you work online.

## Groceries and shopping

Pricing here runs on two forces: local abundance and the cost of distance. Fresh produce, meat, dairy, and tortillas from the Tuesday market and local shops run dramatically cheaper than in the U.S. and taste better for the short trip they made. Imports carry the burden of distance and tariffs, so electronics, clothing, and packaged goods often cost more than back home. For big-box shopping, Querétaro is about an hour away with Costco, Sam's Club, and the large chains, and many residents make a monthly run. The habit that saves daily friction: bring your own bags, because disposable ones are restricted across much of the country.

## Sample monthly costs in San Miguel de Allende

Figures are approximate, in USD, and dated to mid-2026. Ranges are wide on purpose, because neighborhood and lifestyle move them hard. Peso amounts convert at roughly 18 to 20 pesos per dollar.

| Category | Approx. monthly (USD) | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rent, 1BR furnished in or near Centro | $900 to $1,500 | Higher for prime Centro, Guadiana, Ojo de Agua |
| Rent, 2BR outside Centro | $700 to $1,300 | San Antonio, La Lejona, Guadalupe |
| Utilities (electric, gas, water) | $50 to $120 | Little to no AC needed at altitude |
| Home internet (fiber) | $20 to $40 | Often faster than U.S. equivalents |
| Groceries, one person | $250 to $400 | Local produce and meat run cheap |
| Meal out, mid-range for two | $40 to $50 | Inexpensive spots closer to $15 to $20 |
| Domestic help, weekly housekeeper | $60 to $160 | Roughly $3 to $5 an hour |
| Private health insurance, per person | $125 to $250 | About $1,500 to $3,000 a year |

## The bigger picture

Add these together and San Miguel delivers a comfortable, well-supported life for a fraction of what the same life costs in a U.S. metro. The savings are real, and so is the adjustment period.
