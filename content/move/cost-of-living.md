# The Cost of Living in San Miguel de Allende

San Miguel sits in what we came to call the Goldilocks zone, the temperate highlands where highs stay in the low-to-mid 80s, mornings and nights turn crisp, and the weather almost never becomes the obstacle. It is an established expat hub, which means you pay a premium for plug-and-play infrastructure and a ready community. Even so, you will typically save 40 to 50 percent against a comparable U.S. lifestyle, often with a quality-of-life upgrade on top. Here is where the money actually goes.

## Housing

The best long-term rentals here do not appear on the sites you already know. They live in the heads and phones of local realtors who hold quiet access to the good properties, so contact at least two, and weight how connected they are above how much you like them. Standard practice is one month's deposit plus first month's rent upfront. Get the lease in both Spanish and English, and understand that the Spanish version is the one that binds you.

One Mexico-specific wrinkle changes your real monthly cost. Ask who pays for gardening and exterior maintenance (almost always the landlord) and whether an existing housekeeper comes with the house. Rent is usually quoted and paid in pesos, which is what you want, but the exchange rate is a hidden variable. A 35,000-peso cabin cost us $1,400 a month when the dollar bought 25 pesos and $2,060 two years later when the peso strengthened to 17. The walls did not move. Our effective rent did.

Many expat-heavy neighborhoods here are gated fraccionamientos on the edge of town. They deliver security, maintained landscaping, and an instant community, at the cost of walkability and daily immersion. A common pattern is to start in one for the psychological comfort of arrival, then move into a traditional neighborhood once your confidence and Spanish catch up. Both are legitimate choices. Choose on purpose rather than by accident.

## Healthcare

Routine care is where Mexico is at its best, and you pay cash for the clarity of knowing the price before you walk in. Rough figures the book uses: a doctor consultation runs $15 to $25, prescription antibiotics $3 to $8, a basic blood panel $25 to $40, a dental cleaning $25 to $50. That is often less than a U.S. copay before anyone touches a deductible.

San Miguel offers reasonable options backed by the larger city of Querétaro nearby, which matters for anything specialized. For the bigger events, plan a real strategy rather than going bare. One expat cancelled all U.S. coverage to save $400 a month, then a single emergency cost her $23,000. A workable plan usually pays cash for routine care and carries insurance for cross-border emergencies and catastrophic events. Whether a U.S. PPO with international benefits or a comprehensive international plan wins depends on your health history, so get real quotes on your actual situation.

## Domestic help

The math is what makes this reachable. Housekeeping runs 60 to 100 pesos an hour, roughly $3 to $5, with full-day sessions around $25 to $40. Childcare falls in a similar band, maintenance a little higher at 80 to 120 pesos an hour, and basic yard work is often folded into the rent. For the price of a single U.S. deep-clean, you can have help every week for a month, and the hours you buy back are a real part of why the move is worth making.

Budget for one obligation that is easy to miss. Mexican labor law requires an aguinaldo, a holiday bonus of at least 15 days' wages, paid by December 20, and it explicitly covers household staff. Treat it as a real cost, not a tip. Start with referrals from your landlord, who is your single most valuable resource on day one.

## Utilities

Mexican utilities work well, but they run on a logic you learn on purpose. Nobody mails you the manual. Electricity comes from the CFE on a tiered structure that rewards low consumption and turns punishing at the top, so download the app on arrival, register your exact address, and set a reminder to check the balance, because the paper bill really does blow away. Gas is propane from a tank you order by WhatsApp when the gauge nears 20 PSI, roughly 2,000 pesos (about $100) for a full replacement, paid in cash. The water bill is usually bundled into your rent, but treat tap water as not safe to drink and budget for garrafones, the 20-liter jugs, at 30 to 40 pesos each. Home internet is often faster and steadier than what many of us had in U.S. cities, and a UPS for your modem plus a Starlink backup is worth it if you work online.

## Groceries and shopping

Pricing here runs on two forces: local abundance and the cost of distance. Fresh produce, meat, dairy, and tortillas run dramatically cheaper than in the U.S., often well over half off, and taste better because they traveled a short way to reach you. Avocados that cost $2 each in Colorado sell for around 50 cents here. Imports carry the burden of distance and tariffs, so electronics, clothing, and packaged goods often cost more than back home. The move is obvious once you see it: buy local for daily needs, stock up on imports during U.S. visits. One small habit saves daily friction. Bring your own bags, because disposable ones are banned across much of the country.

## The bigger picture

Add these together and San Miguel delivers a comfortable, well-supported life for a fraction of what the same life costs in a U.S. metro. The savings are real, and so is the adjustment period.
