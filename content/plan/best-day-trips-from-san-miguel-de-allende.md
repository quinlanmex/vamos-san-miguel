# Best Day Trips from San Miguel de Allende

The easiest and most rewarding outings are close to home. The Atotonilco sanctuary and the thermal hot springs around it sit about 15 minutes north of town, so you can soak, see a UNESCO-listed church, and be back for dinner. If you want to range farther, historic towns like Dolores Hidalgo, Mineral de Pozos, Guanajuato City, and Queretaro all work as full-day trips. This guide starts with the close, countryside outings the region is known for, then lists bigger trips as options.

## What are the best nearby day trips?

Keep it simple and stay close. The Atotonilco sanctuary paired with a hot spring is the classic half-day, and it captures what the surrounding countryside does best. From there you can add Dolores Hidalgo for its history and ceramics, or Mineral de Pozos for its quiet, semi-ghost-town atmosphere. ## Atotonilco sanctuary and the hot springs

About 15 minutes north of San Miguel lies the Sanctuary of Atotonilco, a church so densely painted inside that it is often called the Sistine Chapel of Mexico. It is part of the same UNESCO World Heritage listing as San Miguel and remains an active pilgrimage site, so dress modestly and be respectful of worshippers.

The sanctuary sits in the middle of hot-spring country. Several thermal springs and spa complexes are clustered within roughly 15 minutes' drive of town, some with warm pools, gardens, and restaurants. A half-day here, church plus a soak plus a slow lunch, is one of the most popular things to do from San Miguel. Bring a swimsuit, a towel, sunscreen, and cash, and go on a weekday if you want the pools quieter. See our Local Picks for which spring to choose.

## Dolores Hidalgo: independence history and ceramics

Dolores Hidalgo is about 40 to 50 minutes north of San Miguel. It is the birthplace of Mexican independence, where the famous 1810 call to arms was made, and today it is known for its Talavera-style ceramics and its unusual ice cream flavors around the main square. Regular buses run there through the day, which makes it an easy trip without a car. Pair it with a countryside route back if you have a driver.

## Mineral de Pozos: a quiet former mining town

Mineral de Pozos is roughly an hour from San Miguel. Once a booming silver-mining town, it emptied out and now draws visitors for its atmospheric ruins, galleries, and slow pace. Public transport is limited, so a car or a private driver is the practical way to go. It suits travelers who want something quiet and offbeat rather than a busy tourist center.

## Bigger day trips: Guanajuato City and Queretaro

**Guanajuato City** is about a 1.5-hour drive and makes a full, rewarding day. The capital of the state is a riot of color built into a ravine, with underground street tunnels, plazas, the Diego Rivera museum, and steep, lively lanes. Give yourself the whole day and consider a guided trip if you would rather not drive the tunnels yourself.

**Queretaro** sits roughly an hour away and has a large, handsome colonial center of its own, with aqueduct views, wide plazas, and a strong food and wine scene. It works well as a full day, and it is also the site of the nearest major airport.

## Should I book a tour or go independently?

For the Atotonilco sanctuary and the hot springs, independent is easy with a short taxi or Uber ride. Dolores Hidalgo is simple by public bus. Mineral de Pozos, Guanajuato City, and Queretaro are more comfortable with a driver or an organized tour, especially if you want to combine stops in one day. ## Frequently asked questions

**What is the closest day trip from San Miguel de Allende?**
The Atotonilco sanctuary and the nearby thermal hot springs, both about 15 minutes north of town. Together they make an ideal half-day.

**Can I visit the hot springs without a car?**
Yes. They are a short, inexpensive taxi or Uber ride from the center. Arrange your return ride in advance or ask the spring's front desk to call one, since the springs are outside town.

**How far is Guanajuato City from San Miguel?**
About a 1.5-hour drive. It is very doable as a full day trip, either self-driven or on a guided tour.

**Is Dolores Hidalgo worth a day trip?**
If you are interested in Mexican independence history, ceramics, or a laid-back small town, yes. It is only about 40 to 50 minutes away and reachable by regular bus.

**What should I bring to the hot springs?**
A swimsuit, a towel, sunscreen, water, and cash. Weekdays are quieter than weekends.

**Which day trips need a car or driver?**
Mineral de Pozos, and any plan that combines several towns in one day, are much easier with a car or a private driver. Guanajuato City and Queretaro are comfortable by organized tour if you would rather not drive.
