# Cost of Living in Mexico: Housing, Healthcare, and Daily Life

Daily life in Mexico typically runs about half of a comparable U.S. lifestyle, and often less. A doctor's visit costs $15 to $25, a housekeeper runs $3 to $5 an hour, a gym membership is $15 to $40 a month, and a walkable rental in a good neighborhood can cost a fraction of your old mortgage. The savings are real, and they come with a set of new systems to learn: how rentals work, how to pay a utility bill at the corner store, and how to decline the ATM's dynamic-currency-conversion trap.

This page walks through what everyday expenses actually cost, category by category, drawing on the finished chapters on housing, healthcare, money, domestic help, utilities, dining, shopping, and transportation. Prices move with the peso and with the season, so treat these as planning ranges rather than quotes.

## How much does housing cost in Mexico?

The best long-term rentals do not appear on Zillow. They live with local realtors who hold quiet access to the most desirable properties, so contact at least two well-connected agents rather than searching only the sites you already know. Standard practice is one month's deposit plus first month's rent, paid upfront, and you should negotiate the deposit-return timeline (landlords often propose 60 days, but 15 days after move-out is more reasonable).

Two housing realities catch Americans off guard:

- **Exchange-rate risk.** Most leases quote and pay in pesos. A 35,000-peso cabin costs about $1,400 when the dollar buys 25 pesos, but $2,060 if the peso strengthens to 17 per dollar. The walls do not move, but your effective rent can swing nearly $700 a month.
- **The Spanish version binds.** A contract needs to be in Spanish to be enforceable, so get the lease in both languages and compare them line by line before signing.

You will also choose between a gated fraccionamiento (controlled security, managed infrastructure, an instant expat community, usually on the outskirts) and a traditional neighborhood (more walkable, more integrated, more daily friction). Many families start in a fraccionamiento for stability and move to a traditional neighborhood once they have the language and confidence to operate independently.

## What does healthcare cost in Mexico?

Routine care in Mexico often costs less than a U.S. copay, before anyone touches a deductible.

| Service | Mexico cost | U.S. copay (before deductible) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Doctor consultation | $15 to $25 | $35 to $50 |
| Prescription antibiotics | $3 to $8 | $10 to $25 |
| Blood work (basic panel) | $25 to $40 | $25 to $50 |
| X-ray | $15 to $30 | $50 to $100 |
| Dental cleaning | $25 to $50 | $50 to $100 |

For larger events, an appendectomy runs roughly $3,000 to $5,000 in Mexico against $15,000 to $25,000 in the U.S., and a normal delivery around $1,500 to $3,000 versus $8,000 to $15,000. One family's emergency CT scan and pediatric visit in Mérida cost $35 total, though the same care in Puerto Vallarta or Mexico City might run $150 to $300. Treat these as orders of magnitude and confirm current prices with the specific hospital.

Your healthcare strategy sits on four separate pillars: local routine care (handled by paying cash), cross-border emergency coverage, specialty and tertiary access, and catastrophic protection. Which insurance pathway fits depends on whether you have pre-existing conditions that need U.S. specialist continuity. Quality clusters around cities and tourist zones, so match where you live to the care you actually need.

## How do payments and money work day to day?

Mexico runs two payment systems at once: cash for the street, cards for the counter. The single easiest money win is at the ATM. When it asks "Accept conversion rate?", choose Decline every time, so your home bank handles the exchange at the true interbank rate instead of a markup of roughly 3% to 7%. Accepting it twice a month can cost you more than $1,000 a year.

A few habits carry the rest:

- **Right-size your bills.** Never hand a street vendor a 500-peso note for a small purchase. Keep 100s, 50s, and 10-peso coins for markets and tips.
- **Build redundancy.** Carry two credit cards and two debit cards from different banks, keep one set at home, notify every bank with a six-month travel window, and keep about 10,000 pesos ($500 to $600) in emergency cash.
- **Use the right U.S. stack.** A zero-foreign-fee credit card (Chase Sapphire, Capital One Venture), a Charles Schwab or Fidelity checking account that reimburses worldwide ATM fees, and Ally for savings.
- **Use Wise for recurring transfers.** Rent, insurance, and utilities move cheaply through Wise at the mid-market rate, since Venmo and Zelle do not work into Mexico without a U.S. bank account on the other end.

## How much does domestic help cost in Mexico?

Handing off the work you dread becomes affordable enough that you can actually do it, and the reclaimed hours are part of the point of moving.

| Service | Typical rate |
| --- | --- |
| Housekeeping | 60 to 100 pesos/hour ($3 to $5); full day 500 to 800 pesos ($25 to $40) |
| Childcare | 60 to 100 pesos/hour ($3 to $5) |
| Maintenance | 80 to 120 pesos/hour ($4 to $6), materials separate |
| Drivers | 100 to 160 pesos/hour ($5 to $8) |
| Yard work | Often included with rent; extra work 60 to 100 pesos/hour ($3 to $5) |

Start with your landlord, who usually comes with trusted providers already attached to the house. One legal point to budget for: Mexico's Federal Labor Law requires an aguinaldo (holiday bonus) of at least 15 days' wages, paid by December 20, and it explicitly covers household staff. Plenty of informal arrangements skip it, but treat it as a real obligation.

## What do utilities cost, and how do you pay them?

Utilities work well but run on a logic you learn on purpose, because nobody mails you the manual.

- **Electricity (CFE).** Billed on a tiered structure that rewards low consumption and climbs steeply as you use more. The paper bill is left in your door and sometimes blows away, so use the CFE app and pay the barcode in cash at any Oxxo.
- **Gas.** Homes run on propane tanks. When the gauge reads 20 PSI, order that day, because you may have hours. A full replacement is around 2,000 pesos (about $100), paid in cash to the driver via a WhatsApp contact.
- **Water.** Usually bundled into rent. Treat tap water as not safe to drink unless you have reverse-osmosis filtration, and buy 20-liter garrafones at 30 to 40 pesos each.
- **Internet.** TelMex, Totalplay, Izzi, and Megacable cover home service, often faster than U.S. cable. For remote workers in smaller towns, Starlink moves from luxury to necessity, though the cross-border billing setup usually needs a Mexican card or a helpful local to place the first order.

Back your essential devices with a UPS, which both bridges short outages and smooths the brownouts that would otherwise crash your router.

## What about food, dining, and shopping?

Local abundance drives the savings, and distance drives the costs. Fresh produce, meat, dairy, and tortillas often run well over half off U.S. prices (an avocado is around 50 cents), while electronics, clothing, and imported packaged goods usually cost more, so stock up on imports during U.S. visits and buy local for daily needs.

- **Dining out.** Tip 10% as the baseline and 15% as the appreciated default, not the 20% to 25% U.S. reflex. Wine that runs $15 to $20 in the States sells for $8 to $12 here.
- **Fitness.** Neighborhood gyms run 300 to 800 pesos a month ($15 to $40) against $150 or more in a major U.S. city, and personal training is often 200 to 400 pesos a session ($10 to $20).
- **Bags.** Disposable plastic and paper bags are banned across much of the country, so bring your own or buy them at the register.
- **Pharmacies.** Function like small health clinics, with many U.S.-prescription drugs available over the counter (antibiotics are the exception, requiring a prescription since 2010) and generics far cheaper than U.S. equivalents.

## Do I need a car, and what does transportation cost?

Often you do not, and buying one too fast is the most common transportation mistake expats make. Spend your first month car-free and let your actual routine decide.

| Item | Cost |
| --- | --- |
| Uber, CDMX airport to Roma Norte | $12 to $15 (30 to 40% below U.S. rates) |
| Luxury bus, CDMX to San Miguel de Allende | $38 to $45 |
| Mexico City metro ride | 5 pesos (about a quarter) |
| Full-coverage car insurance, mid-size SUV | $70 to $150+ per month |
| Flat tire repair at a vulcanizadora | $6 to $8 |

Big cities reward going car-free (Uber, InDrive, metro, walking); small and mountain towns usually reward owning. Your valid U.S. driver's license is recognized for tourist and short-term driving with no translation needed. If you do drive, Mexican auto insurance is legally required, because the government does not recognize U.S. or Canadian policies as valid proof of liability, and premiums typically run 30 to 50% below comparable U.S. coverage.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does it cost to live in Mexico per month?**
Daily life typically runs about half of a comparable U.S. lifestyle. Concrete anchors: a doctor's visit is $15 to $25, a housekeeper is $3 to $5 an hour, a gym is $15 to $40 a month, an Uber airport run is $12 to $15, and household utilities are paid in cash at the corner store. Housing varies most and swings with the peso exchange rate.

**Is healthcare in Mexico cheaper than the U.S.?**
Yes, often dramatically. Routine care frequently costs less than a U.S. copay before any deductible (a $15 to $25 visit, $3 to $8 antibiotics), and an appendectomy runs roughly $3,000 to $5,000 versus $15,000 to $25,000 in the U.S. Match your city to the care you need, since quality clusters around larger cities and tourist zones.

**How do I avoid overpaying at Mexican ATMs?**
When the machine asks "Accept conversion rate?", press Decline so your home bank handles the exchange at the true interbank rate. Accepting the offered rate adds roughly 3% to 7%, which can cost you over $1,000 a year. Use ATMs attached to bank branches and withdraw larger amounts less often.

**Do I need to speak Spanish to rent an apartment?**
It helps, and you should plan for it. The Spanish version of a lease is the legally binding one, so get the contract in both languages and compare them line by line before signing. Work with well-connected local realtors, who hold access to the best properties that never reach the listing sites.

**Do I need a car to live in Mexico?**
Not necessarily. In big cities a car often sits unused while you take Ubers (30 to 40% below U.S. rates) and the metro (5 pesos a ride). Small and mountain towns usually justify ownership. Spend your first month car-free and let your routine decide. Your U.S. license works for short-term driving, but Mexican auto insurance is legally required if you drive.
