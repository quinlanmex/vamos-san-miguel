# The Money Case for Moving to Mexico

If you earn in dollars and spend in pesos, moving to Mexico can save you somewhere between $50,000 and $130,000 a year through three stacked levers: the Foreign Earned Income Exclusion on your federal taxes, a no-tax-state domicile on your state taxes, and a cost of living that runs roughly half of what you pay in the U.S. A $75,000 salary in Austin buys the purchasing power of $125,000 or more once you are living in Mexico. Every year you wait costs you the full spread, potentially $148,500 in combined tax and lifestyle savings, money that could otherwise be compounding in an index fund.

This page pulls the financial argument together from across the book. Read it to understand the shape of the case, then go to the member chapters for the mechanics and the caveats, because the numbers here are frameworks and estimates, not a quote for your specific situation.

## How much can you actually save by moving to Mexico?

The savings come from three separate places, and they add up rather than overlap.

- **Federal taxes.** The Foreign Earned Income Exclusion (FEIE) eliminates most or all of your U.S. federal income tax on earned income for a great many expats. You qualify through the Physical Presence Test by spending 330 days outside the U.S. in any rolling 12-month window.
- **State taxes.** Establishing a domicile in a no-income-tax state (Florida, Texas, Nevada, and the like) before you leave removes your state income tax on remote-work income.
- **Cost of living.** Daily expenses in Mexico typically run about half of a comparable U.S. lifestyle, and often lower.

Layer those together and the full-time configuration described in the book saves a representative earner somewhere between $50,000 and $130,000 a year. The exact figure depends entirely on your income, your entity structure, and where you settle.

## What does waiting a year really cost?

Delay is the most expensive line item, because the savings you skip could have been invested. At a 10.33% annual return, the price of putting the move off climbs fast.

| How long you wait | What the delay costs you |
| --- | --- |
| 1 year | $148,500 (the first year's missed savings) |
| 5 years | $220,040 |
| 10 years | $359,724 |

Put another way, roughly $12,375 a month in potential savings evaporates while you think it over. That is the compounding cost of standing still.

## How does the purchasing power math work?

The arbitrage is simple arithmetic: you keep a dollar income and move it to a peso cost structure. A $75,000 salary in Austin buys the purchasing power of $125,000 or more in Mexico. One remote marketing manager profiled in the book converted her role to remote and moved to Mexico City, where her $85,000 salary gave her the lifestyle of someone earning $140,000 or more in Seattle.

There is a time dividend too. The average American spends 54 minutes a day commuting, about 220 hours a year, close to six full work weeks, getting to a place to do work that could happen from a kitchen or a terrace. Remote workers were 13% more productive than their in-office counterparts in a Stanford study, so the location change tends to help output rather than hurt it.

## What about the equity locked in my house?

Home appreciation is paper wealth until you move it somewhere cheaper. If your $600,000 house was worth $400,000 five years ago, that $200,000 gain only becomes real if you downsize or relocate, because buying a similar house in the same appreciated market gives back the gain at the closing table.

Freeing that capital changes the picture. A $200,000 equity gain invested at a long-run market return near 10% grows to about $326,000 in five years and $533,000 in ten, while your monthly spending falls. You have two ways to reach it: rent out your U.S. home for roughly twice what a comparable place costs you in Mexico, or sell, bank the equity, and rent in Mexico while the capital compounds.

The mortgage-interest-deduction objection does not hold up here. Pay $20,000 a year in interest in the 24% bracket and the deduction saves you $4,800 in taxes, which still leaves you out $15,200 in interest. The deduction earns its keep when you need the housing and can afford the payment, not as a reason to hold a house while better options sit in plain view.

## What the money case does not eliminate

Preserve these caveats, because they are the difference between a plan and a fantasy.

- **FEIE covers your U.S. taxes only.** Whether you owe Mexican income tax is a separate, genuinely open question that depends on your residency status under Mexican law, your entity structure, and where your work is physically performed. Decide it deliberately with a cross-border CPA, not by inheriting your neighbor's assumption.
- **Self-employment tax survives.** Neither the FEIE nor the U.S.-Mexico tax treaty eliminates U.S. self-employment tax (Social Security and Medicare). It applies to your worldwide self-employment income no matter where you live. Budget for it.
- **These are frameworks, not quotes.** The savings percentages, arbitrage tiers, and delay figures are the authors' estimates and planning models. Your real numbers come from your own facts and your own advisors.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does it cost of living run in Mexico compared to the U.S.?**
Daily expenses typically run about half of a comparable U.S. lifestyle, and the book's full-time configuration saves a representative earner $50,000 to $130,000 a year once you add federal tax, state tax, and cost-of-living reductions together. The exact figure depends on your income and where you settle.

**Do I still pay U.S. taxes if I move to Mexico?**
The Foreign Earned Income Exclusion eliminates most or all federal income tax on earned income if you spend 330 days a year outside the U.S., and a no-tax-state domicile removes state income tax. U.S. self-employment tax still applies, and whether you owe Mexican income tax is a separate question to settle with a cross-border CPA.

**How much does waiting to move cost me?**
Roughly $12,375 a month, or about $148,500 in the first year, in combined tax and lifestyle savings you could otherwise invest. At a 10.33% return, a ten-year delay costs around $359,724.

**Should I sell my house or rent it out before moving?**
Both work. Renting out a typical U.S. home brings in roughly twice what a comparable place costs you in Mexico while you keep building equity. Selling frees the equity to compound: a $200,000 gain invested near 10% grows to about $326,000 in five years and $533,000 in ten.

**Does keeping my mortgage for the interest deduction make financial sense?**
Usually not on its own. A $20,000 interest bill in the 24% bracket saves you $4,800 in tax and still leaves you out $15,200. The deduction is worth having when you need the housing, not as a reason to hold a house while better uses of the capital sit available.
